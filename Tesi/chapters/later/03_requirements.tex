\chapter{Requirements}
\label{chap:requirements}

This chapter lists what FlowSync must do and the qualities it must have. The requirements were
derived from the analysis of the application domains (Section~\ref{sec:domain}) and refined
while designing the user interface: the HTML mockups of all the screens were written first,
reviewed once by the team, and used as the reference for the functional requirements. The
chapter also records, for each requirement, how far the three parts of the system have come at
the time of writing (25 November 2026).

\section{Elicitation and main decisions}
\label{sec:elicitation}

The requirements come from three sources: the common model of
Section~\ref{sec:common-model}, which fixes the entities and the constraints; the mockups of
the user interface, in which every screen corresponds to a task of a user; and a few decisions
taken by the team while reviewing the mockups. The most important of these decisions shape the
rest of the system.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Three plans for every request.} A request does not produce a single plan but
        three alternatives that span the trade-off between time and cost: an \emph{economic}
        plan (slower, no overtime), a \emph{balanced} plan (chosen with the weights of the
        planner) and a \emph{fast} plan (it finishes earlier and costs more). The planner
        chooses among concrete alternatives instead of tuning weights in the abstract.
  \item \textbf{Comparison with the classical rules.} Every request is also solved with the ATC
        and EDD dispatching rules, and their indicators are shown next to the plans of the AI.
        The planner can see at a glance whether the AI is doing better than a simple rule.
  \item \textbf{The planner decides.} No plan is executed without an explicit approval; a plan
        can also be modified by hand on the Gantt chart, and the modified version is checked
        again against the constraints.
  \item \textbf{Known problems are not asked again.} When an operator reports a problem (for
        example a machine breakdown), the rescheduling starts from the report: the planner sees
        the problem and its impact on the running plan, and only adjusts a few parameters.
  \item \textbf{Italian user interface.} The users are planners and operators of Italian
        organisations, so the interface is in Italian; code, contracts and this document are in
        English or Italian according to the audience.
\end{itemize}

\section{Actors}
\label{sec:actors}

FlowSync has three human actors, which correspond to the roles of the system, and two system
actors.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{planner} (role \texttt{PLANNER}) enters the orders and their process graph,
        requests the plans, compares them, modifies them, approves or rejects them, and
        reschedules when something goes wrong.
  \item The \textbf{operator} (role \texttt{OPERATOR}) works on the shop floor or in an office:
        sees the activities of the day, records when each one starts, pauses and ends, and
        reports problems.
  \item The \textbf{administrator} (role \texttt{ADMIN}) manages the master data (resources,
        calendars, skills, domain profiles), approves the registrations of new users and
        consults the audit log.
  \item The \textbf{AI service} receives the problem specifications from the backend and returns
        the plans; it is never visible to the users.
  \item The \textbf{training pipeline} trains and evaluates the models offline and publishes a
        new version only if it is not worse than the dispatching rules.
\end{itemize}
A person may have more than one role. Everyone who is not authenticated can only log in or
register; a new account stays pending until an administrator approves it and assigns a role.

\section{Non-functional requirements}
\label{sec:nfr}

\begin{table}[htpb]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lp{3cm}p{9cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{ID} & \textbf{Quality} & \textbf{Requirement} \\
    \midrule
    NFR-01 & Correctness & Every plan returned to the planner is feasible: precedences,
             capacities, availability and fixed activities are respected. It is enforced by
             construction and checked by an independent verifier. \\
    NFR-02 & Responsiveness & A request is accepted immediately and computed in the
             background; the plans are ready in a few seconds, so that the planner can
             replan several times a day. \\
    NFR-03 & Human control & No plan is executed without the approval of a planner; a
             modified plan is validated again by the backend. \\
    NFR-04 & Traceability & Every plan records its input, the model version and the
             decision taken; the input is stored as a snapshot and identified by a hash, so
             each plan can be reproduced. \\
    NFR-05 & Security & Authentication with JSON Web Tokens and authorisation by role on every
             call; the AI service is reachable only from the internal network. \\
    NFR-06 & Robustness & A slow or unavailable AI service does not block the backend (time
             limit, circuit breaker); a fallback always returns a plan when one exists. \\
    NFR-07 & Usability & Interface in Italian, errors shown next to the field that caused
             them, keyboard alternatives to drag and drop, contrast and target sizes in line
             with WCAG~2.1 AA. The interface is designed for desktop screens. \\
    NFR-08 & Generality & The same system serves factory, public administration and project
             scheduling, by changing the domain profile and not the code. \\
    NFR-09 & Maintainability & The parts communicate only through versioned contracts, and
             each can be developed and tested alone against a mock of the others. \\
    NFR-10 & Portability & The whole system starts with Docker Compose in the same way on
             every machine. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
