\section{Parallel lanes and dependencies}
\label{sec:lanes}

From 9 November the three development lanes, AI, backend and frontend, run in parallel, each
owned by one member of the team (Section~\ref{sec:contributions}). The lanes depend on each
other only through the contracts (Section~\ref{sec:contracts}), and each lane starts against a
mock of the next one: the frontend against a mock of the backend, the backend against a mock of
the AI service. In this way no lane waits for another, and the integration is a sequence of
small steps, in which a mock is replaced by the real component one endpoint at a time. The
Christmas break runs from 24 December to 7 January. The dates of the chart are indicative: the
plan is revised at each milestone (Section~\ref{sec:process}).

\section{Risks}
\label{sec:risks}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The learned policy does not beat the rules.} This is the most uncertain part of
        the project, and the reason why milestone M1 is a go/no-go decision placed early. If the
        pilot model does not beat ATC, the system still works with the dispatching rules as
        optimisation engine, and the effort moves to the features of the policy and to the data.
  \item \textbf{Integration delays.} Three parts developed in parallel may diverge. The risk is
        reduced by the contracts agreed before the code, by mocks that follow the contracts
        exactly, and by updating the contracts as soon as a part needs something new.
  \item \textbf{Missing real data.} For some domains no reliable public data exist; the models
        are trained on synthetic instances, and public benchmarks are used to calibrate and to
        validate them.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} The feature freeze (M3) leaves two weeks before the delivery for testing,
        fixes and documentation.
\end{itemize}
